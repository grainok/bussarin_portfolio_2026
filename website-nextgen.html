01 / CONTEXT

A global platform. A local experience.

NextGen Commerce brought together shopping, promotions, subscriptions, registration and post-purchase journeys, with Thailand as the pilot market. I collaborated with the global team and Thailand stakeholders on UX/UI design, reusable components, research activities, usability testing and UAT.

02 / UNDERSTAND

Start with people and their tasks.

Research activities included workshops, internal sessions and in-depth interviews, followed by usability testing of purchase, A Point, order and account journeys. Further testing focused on SOP Manual in the cart. Staff and Amway Business Owners (ABOs) helped the team examine the experience in a local context. Across the user-testing programme, 40 users participated in rounds organised into groups of 8. The June 2024 co-creation report below documents one 8-person round.

User research

Explore user needs, existing journeys and local expectations through workshops and interviews.

Define the problem

Review friction around promotions, coupon visibility, delivery fees, cart controls and payment or order states.

Three findings that informed the design work

01 / CART NAVIGATION

A clear route back to shopping

What we observed

Participants could not easily find how to leave the cart and add more products. Some expected an “Add More Products” action.

Design recommendation

Make the route to continued shopping visible. The report recommends adding bottom navigation.

View study evidence

02 / PROMOTION DISCOVERABILITY

Put special-price products where people look

What we observed

Participants overlooked the special-price product selection at the top of the cart and suggested a lower position.

Design recommendation

Review promotion placement near the coupon section at the bottom of the screen, as recommended in the study.

View study evidence

03 / IDENTITY VERIFICATION

Make the verification action understandable

What we observed

The registration findings included uncertainty about the Verify link and why identity checking was required.

Design recommendation

Give verification a more prominent button and explain the purpose of the identity check with visible supporting text.

View study evidence

04 / DESIGN & PROTOTYPE

Build clarity into screens and components.

Work with the global team to translate local requirements into screen structure, interaction states and reusable components. Use prototypes and reviews to connect global consistency with Thai content and commerce rules.

The selected designs show Homepage, Product Category, Promotion, SOP, About Amway and Registration / ABO / Member views, with product cards, campaign tiles and mobile navigation.

BETA 2 / DESIGN RESPONSE

Translate feedback into clearer interactions.

The Beta 2 recommendation deck compares the existing Thailand website with NextGen journeys and proposes UI changes informed by Beta 1, Beta 2 and ABO co-creation feedback.

Make SOP easier to discover

Propose a first-level SOP entry point in desktop navigation and mobile navigation, addressing feedback that users struggled to locate subscription programmes.

Clarify subscription choices

Propose “Subscribe Now” wording, visible plan benefits, clearer frequency and duration choices, and stronger selection states on the product page.

FLOW SIMPLIFICATION / PROPOSED DESIGN

Reduce unnecessary steps in proxy ordering

The deck maps the NextGen downline proxy-cart journey at 8 clicks and proposes a 4-click flow: make the whole cart-switch area clickable, expose downline search directly and remove an unnecessary step. A separate downline subscription proposal maps a change from 7 pages / 13 clicks to 5 pages / 11 clicks.

These are design-level step counts in the recommendation deck (pages 6–7 and 18–19). They describe proposed flows, not measured completion times or verified post-release improvements.
